TITLE: Golden Throne of Tutankhamun – Overview
LANGUAGE: en
MUSEUM: GEM
EXHIBIT: Golden Throne
PERIOD: New Kingdom, Dynasty 18 (Tutankhamun)
TYPE: overview
TAGS: Tutankhamun, Golden Throne, royal furniture, New Kingdom

TEXT:
The Golden Throne of Tutankhamun is a richly decorated ceremonial armchair found wrapped in linen in the Antechamber of his tomb. Made of wood covered with sheets of gold and inlaid with colored materials, it is one of the most famous royal objects from the 18th Dynasty.



ID: gem_tut_golden_throne_details_en_002
TITLE: Golden Throne of Tutankhamun – Materials and Decoration
LANGUAGE: en
MUSEUM: GEM
EXHIBIT: Golden Throne
PERIOD: New Kingdom, Dynasty 18 (Tutankhamun)
TYPE: details
TAGS: gold sheets, faience, semi-precious stones, inlay, uraeus

TEXT:
The throne is constructed from wood covered with gold sheets that are chased and inlaid with glass, faience, and semi-precious stones. Its side panels are formed by winged uraeus snakes holding the king’s cartouche, combining luxury craftsmanship with strong protective symbolism.



ID: gem_tut_golden_throne_scene_en_003
TITLE: Golden Throne of Tutankhamun – The Backrest Scene
LANGUAGE: en
MUSEUM: GEM
EXHIBIT: Golden Throne
PERIOD: New Kingdom, Dynasty 18 (Tutankhamun)
TYPE: significance
TAGS: Aten, royal couple, Amarna, restoration

TEXT:
A celebrated scene on the backrest shows the king and queen under a floral canopy, with rays of the Aten sun disk reaching down toward them. This imagery reflects the transitional ideology early in Tutankhamun’s reign, bridging Amarna-style symbolism with the return to traditional royal authority.



ID: gem_tut_golden_throne_context_en_004
TITLE: Golden Throne of Tutankhamun – Historical Context
LANGUAGE: en
MUSEUM: GEM
EXHIBIT: Golden Throne
PERIOD: New Kingdom, Dynasty 18 (Tutankhamun)
TYPE: historical_background
TAGS: post-Amarna, Akhenaten reforms, traditional religion

TEXT:
Tutankhamun’s reign followed the religious reforms of Akhenaten, and many objects from his early years show a complex mix of styles and beliefs. The throne offers insight into this cultural shift and the restoration of traditional religious and political order in late Dynasty 18.

Q: Why are Aten rays shown on the throne?
A: It reflects early reign imagery influenced by the Amarna period.

Q: What do the winged uraei mean?
A: They symbolize royal protection.

Q: Was it used as daily furniture?
A: It was likely ceremonial and symbolic, not ordinary seating.
