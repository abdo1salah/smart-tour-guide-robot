TYPE: details
TAGS: granite, nemes headdress, sema-tawy, Egyptian art

TEXT:
Senwosert I is shown wearing the traditional nemes headdress and seated on a throne decorated with the sema-tawy symbol, representing the unification of Upper and Lower Egypt. The statue’s balanced proportions, smooth surfaces, and controlled posture reflect the Middle Kingdom ideal of harmony, order, and disciplined kingship.

PERIOD: Middle Kingdom, Dynasty 12 (c. 1971–1926 BCE)
TYPE: significance
TAGS: funerary temple, royal power, cosmic order, Middle Kingdom ideology

TEXT:
This statue was one of a group of ten similar figures placed in the king’s funerary temple. Together, they acted as conduits of royal power, ensuring the king’s eternal presence and protecting cosmic order. Temple visitors would have encountered these statues as visual expressions of stability and continuity.
