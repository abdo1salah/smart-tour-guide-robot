PERIOD: New Kingdom, Dynasty 18 (Tutankhamun)
TYPE: overview
TAGS: Tutankhamun, canopic coffinette, Nephthys, funerary gold

TEXT:
These miniature anthropoid coffins were created to hold Tutankhamun’s embalmed internal organs. Made of solid beaten gold, each coffinette reflects the high level of craftsmanship and religious importance placed on preserving the king’s body for eternity.

Each coffinette is decorated with intricate feathered patterns inlaid with red carnelian and colored glass. The king is shown wearing the nemes headcloth with both the vulture and uraeus emblems, and holding the crook and flail, symbols of royal authority and divine kingship.

TITLE: Canopic Coffinette of Tutankhamun – Function and Protection
LANGUAGE: en
MUSEUM: GEM
EXHIBIT: Canopic Coffinette
PERIOD: New Kingdom, Dynasty 18 (Tutankhamun)
TYPE: significance
TAGS: embalmed organs, Four Sons of Horus, funerary theology

TEXT:
Canopic coffinettes served as sacred containers for the king’s preserved organs, which were believed essential for spiritual renewal. Each coffinette was associated with one of the Four Sons of Horus and a protective goddess; in this case, Nephthys, who provided divine protection.

TEXT:
The luxurious use of gold and inlaid materials reflects both the wealth of the royal workshops and the theological importance of safeguarding the king’s physical essence. These coffinettes formed part of a carefully structured funerary system designed to ensure resurrection and eternal life.
