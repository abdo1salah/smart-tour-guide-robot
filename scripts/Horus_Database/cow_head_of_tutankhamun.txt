TITLE: Cow Head – Overview
LANGUAGE: en
MUSEUM: GEM
EXHIBIT: Cow Head
PERIOD: New Kingdom, Dynasty 18 (Tutankhamun)
TYPE: overview
TAGS: Hathor, Tutankhamun, funerary symbolism, cow imagery

TEXT:
This ceremonial cow’s head was carved from a single block of wood and found with its throat wrapped in linen. Parts of the head are covered in gold foil, while others are painted black, creating a striking ritual appearance.

TITLE: Cow Head – Materials and Craftsmanship
LANGUAGE: en
MUSEUM: GEM
EXHIBIT: Cow Head
PERIOD: New Kingdom, Dynasty 18 (Tutankhamun)
TYPE: details
TAGS: gold foil, wood carving, ceremonial objects

TEXT:
The upper neck, head, and ears are gilded, while the lower neck is painted black. The wooden horns are coated in copper and thick black resin. These materials enhanced both the visual impact and the symbolic power of the object.



ID: gem_tut_cow_head_function_en_003
TITLE: Cow Head – Religious Meaning
LANGUAGE: en
MUSEUM: GEM
EXHIBIT: Cow Head
PERIOD: New Kingdom, Dynasty 18 (Tutankhamun)
TYPE: significance
TAGS: Hathor, afterlife, funerary belief, Thebes

TEXT:
The cow is associated with the goddess Hathor, who welcomed the dead into the afterlife and was often shown emerging from the western mountains. This object reflects longstanding funerary beliefs linking Hathor to rebirth and divine guidance.
