TITLE: Hanging Obelisk of Ramesses II – Overview
LANGUAGE: en
MUSEUM: GEM
EXHIBIT: Hanging Obelisk of Ramesses II
PERIOD: New Kingdom – 19th Dynasty (Reign of Ramesses II, 1279–1213 BCE)
TYPE: overview
TAGS: Ramesses II, obelisk, Tanis, New Kingdom, monumental architecture

TEXT:
This red granite obelisk of Ramesses II once stood at Tanis (San el-Hagar) in Egypt’s Eastern Delta and is the tallest obelisk known from that city, reaching about 16 meters in height. The monument weighs roughly 87 tons and bears inscriptions on all four sides naming the Horus name, throne name, and birth name of the king. Today it is displayed at the Grand Egyptian Museum in Giza as the famous “Hanging Obelisk,” suspended above a platform so visitors can view the cartouches carved on its lower section.



ID: gem_ramesses2_obelisk_details_en_002
TITLE: Hanging Obelisk of Ramesses II – Materials and Artistic Features
LANGUAGE: en
MUSEUM: GEM
EXHIBIT: Hanging Obelisk of Ramesses II
PERIOD: New Kingdom – 19th Dynasty
TYPE: details
TAGS: red granite, hieroglyphs, cartouches, pyramidion

TEXT:
The obelisk is carved from red granite quarried in Aswan. Its four sides are covered with hieroglyphic inscriptions that praise Ramesses II and record his royal titles. The polished surfaces demonstrate the high level of skill achieved by ancient Egyptian stoneworkers. At the top of the obelisk is a pyramidion, originally designed to reflect sunlight and symbolize the rays of the sun.



ID: gem_ramesses2_obelisk_function_en_003
TITLE: Hanging Obelisk of Ramesses II – Function / Purpose
LANGUAGE: en
MUSEUM: GEM
EXHIBIT: Hanging Obelisk of Ramesses II
PERIOD: New Kingdom – 19th Dynasty
TYPE: significance
TAGS: temple monument, solar symbolism, Amun-Re worship

TEXT:
Obelisks were monumental structures typically placed at the entrances of temples. They symbolized the rays of the sun and were closely associated with the sun god Ra and the god Amun-Re. By erecting such monuments, Ramesses II proclaimed his devotion to the gods and reinforced his divine authority as pharaoh.

MUSEUM: GEM
EXHIBIT: Hanging Obelisk of Ramesses II
PERIOD: New Kingdom – 19th Dynasty
TYPE: historical_background
TAGS: Ramesses II, Tanis monuments, imperial Egypt

TEXT:
Ramesses II commissioned numerous monuments throughout Egypt during his long reign. At Tanis alone he raised more than ten obelisks, demonstrating his dedication to temple building and royal propaganda. These monuments celebrated the king’s victories, power, and relationship with the gods during Egypt’s imperial peak.

MUSEUM: GEM

PERIOD: New Kingdom – 19th Dynasty
TYPE: historical_background
TAGS: Tanis, San el-Hagar, Eastern Delta

TEXT:
The obelisk originally stood at Tanis (modern San el-Hagar) in Egypt’s Eastern Nile Delta. Tanis was an important religious and political center during later periods of Egyptian history, where many monuments from earlier dynasties were reused or relocated.



ID: gem_ramesses2_obelisk_discovery_en_006
TITLE: Hanging Obelisk of Ramesses II – Discovery / Archaeology
LANGUAGE: en
MUSEUM: GEM
EXHIBIT: Hanging Obelisk of Ramesses II
PERIOD: Modern era
TYPE: historical_background
TAGS: Tanis excavations, archaeology, museum relocation

TEXT:
Fragments of obelisks from Tanis were discovered during archaeological excavations beginning in the late nineteenth century. The obelisk now displayed at the Grand Egyptian Museum was recovered and carefully transported for conservation and display. It was installed in a special suspended structure at the museum entrance, allowing visitors to view inscriptions on its base that were previously hidden.



ID: gem_ramesses2_obelisk_symbolism_en_007
TITLE: Hanging Obelisk of Ramesses II – Symbolism or Meaning
LANGUAGE: en
MUSEUM: GEM
EXHIBIT: Hanging Obelisk of Ramesses II
PERIOD: New Kingdom – 19th Dynasty
TYPE: significance
TAGS: sun symbolism, benben stone, divine kingship

TEXT:
The obelisk symbolized the benben stone, the sacred mound of creation associated with the sun god Ra. Its tall form represented rays of sunlight descending from the heavens. The inscriptions linking Ramesses II with the gods reinforced the idea that the king ruled with divine approval and maintained cosmic order, known as maat.
