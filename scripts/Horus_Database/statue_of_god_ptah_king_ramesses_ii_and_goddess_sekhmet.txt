Exceptional anatomical detailing—prominent collarbones, musculature, and kneecaps reflect masterful craftsmanship of New Kingdom artists.

PERIOD: New Kingdom – 19th Dynasty (Reign of Ramesses II, c. 1279–1213 BCE)
TYPE: historical_background
TAGS: Ramesses II, Ptah, Sekhmet, Memphite Triad, temple art, New Kingdom religion

TEXT:
Ramesses II, one of Egypt’s most powerful pharaohs, ruled during a period of architectural grandeur and intense temple building devoted to both himself and Egypt’s primary gods. Ptah was the creator god of Memphis, patron of craftsmen. Sekhmet, the warrior and healing goddess, was his consort. Together, they formed part of the Memphite Triad (Ptah–Sekhmet–Nefertum), embodying kingship, creation, and protection. The statue reflects New Kingdom religious philosophy, in which the pharaoh’s divine essence was continually reinforced through temple art.

TEXT:
Q: Where can I see this statue?
A: It is displayed along the Grand Stairs of the Grand Egyptian Museum in Giza.

Q: How large is it?
A: Approximately 3.5 meters tall and carved entirely from a single block of red granite.

Q: Who was Ramesses II?
A: One of Egypt’s most famous pharaohs (reigning 1279–1213 BCE), known for massive building projects and celebrated as “Ramesses the Great.”

Q: Why is Sekhmet often paired with Ptah?
A: She is his divine consort; together, they represent two forces of creation—craftsmanship (Ptah) and powerful energy (Sekhmet).

Q: Was the statue restored or altered?
A: Yes, inscriptions indicate Sekhmet’s figure replaced an older depiction of Harsaphes, showing adaptation of older religious forms.
