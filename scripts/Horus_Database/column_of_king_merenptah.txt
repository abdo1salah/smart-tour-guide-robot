TITLE: Column of King Merenptah – Materials and Reliefs
LANGUAGE: en
MUSEUM: GEM
EXHIBIT: Column of King Merenptah
PERIOD: New Kingdom, Dynasty 19 (c. 1213–1203 BCE)
TYPE: details
TAGS: relief carving, hieroglyphs, tripartite base, Egyptian art

TEXT:
The column features a tripartite base made of limestone and sandstone, topped by a red granite shaft. Carved reliefs show King Merenptah receiving weapons from the gods and triumphing over defeated enemies. Scenes of bound prisoners and captured spoils are accompanied by celebratory hieroglyphic inscriptions.



ID: gem_merenptah_column_function_en_003
TITLE: Column of King Merenptah – Function and Meaning
LANGUAGE: en
MUSEUM: GEM
EXHIBIT: Column of King Merenptah
PERIOD: New Kingdom, Dynasty 19 (c. 1213–1203 BCE)
TYPE: significance
TAGS: military victories, divine sanction, royal propaganda, temple monuments

TEXT:
The column was erected to commemorate Merenptah’s military victories, particularly against the Libyans and the Sea Peoples. By depicting the king receiving weapons from the gods, the monument emphasizes that his success in battle was divinely sanctioned and essential to maintaining cosmic order.

LANGUAGE: en

EXHIBIT: Column of King Merenptah
PERIOD: New Kingdom, Dynasty 19 (c. 1213–1203 BCE)
TYPE: historical_background
TAGS: Heliopolis, temple context, New Kingdom religion

TEXT:
Originally erected at Heliopolis, a major religious center associated with the sun god Ra, the column stood in a sacred landscape that reinforced royal and divine authority. Its placement linked Merenptah’s victories with the favor of the gods worshipped at one of Egypt’s most important cult centers.



ID: gem_merenptah_column_history_en_005
TITLE: Column of King Merenptah – Historical Background
LANGUAGE: en
MUSEUM: GEM
EXHIBIT: Column of King Merenptah
PERIOD: New Kingdom, Dynasty 19 (c. 1213–1203 BCE)
TYPE: historical_background
TAGS: son of Ramesses II, Israel Stele, Ramesside period, military campaigns

TEXT:
Merenptah was the son and successor of Ramesses II and ruled Egypt during a period of external threats. His reign is best known for military campaigns commemorated in monumental inscriptions, including the famous Israel Stele. The imagery on this column reflects a late Ramesside emphasis on conquest and divine kingship.
