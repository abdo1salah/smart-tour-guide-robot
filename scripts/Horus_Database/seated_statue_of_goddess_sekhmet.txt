TEXT:
A well-preserved granodiorite statue depicting the lion-headed goddess Sekhmet seated on a throne with a pillar-back. She holds an ankh symbol of life in her left hand while her right hand rests on her knee. The statue originally had a solar disk crown on her head, now missing. Hieroglyphic inscriptions on the throne link the statue to the reign of Pharaoh Amenhotep III.

TYPE: details

TEXT:
The statue is carved from hard granodiorite, a durable dark igneous stone commonly used for royal sculptures. Sekhmet is shown with a realistic lioness head, detailed musculature, and a symmetrical seated pose. She wears a broad collar and holds the ankh symbol of life. Hieroglyphic inscriptions on the throne connect the statue to the pharaoh Amenhotep III and his cult.



ID: gem_seated_sekhmet_function_en_003

LANGUAGE: en
MUSEUM: GEM
EXHIBIT: Seated Statue of Goddess Sekhmet
PERIOD: New Kingdom – 18th Dynasty
TYPE: significance
TAGS: temple rituals, votive statue, sacred lake, royal protection, healing rituals

TEXT:
The statue functioned as a votive and ritual object within temple complexes. Many statues of Sekhmet were placed in rows around sacred lakes where priests performed daily rituals intended to pacify the goddess. These rituals sought to transform her destructive power into healing protection for the pharaoh and the kingdom.



ID: gem_seated_sekhmet_history_en_004
TITLE: Seated Statue of Goddess Sekhmet – Historical Context
LANGUAGE: en
MUSEUM: GEM
EXHIBIT: Seated Statue of Goddess Sekhmet
PERIOD: New Kingdom – 18th Dynasty (Reign of Amenhotep III)
TYPE: historical_background
TAGS: Amenhotep III, Sekhmet statues, Egyptian empire, Eye of Ra

TEXT:
During the reign of Amenhotep III, Egypt experienced a period of great prosperity and imperial power. The king commissioned hundreds of statues of Sekhmet, possibly over 365, reflecting his devotion to the goddess and the importance of maintaining divine protection. Sekhmet represented both destructive power as the "Eye of Ra" and healing energy that could protect the pharaoh and restore balance.



ID: gem_seated_sekhmet_location_en_005
TITLE: Seated Statue of Goddess Sekhmet – Original Location
LANGUAGE: en
MUSEUM: GEM
EXHIBIT: Seated Statue of Goddess Sekhmet
PERIOD: New Kingdom – 18th Dynasty
TYPE: historical_background
TAGS: Temple of Mut, Karnak, Thebes, Kom el-Hetan

TEXT:
Many statues of Sekhmet were originally placed in the Temple of Mut at Karnak in ancient Thebes (modern Luxor). Some scholars suggest that several statues were initially created for Amenhotep III’s mortuary temple at Kom el-Hetan before being relocated to other temple complexes.

MUSEUM: GEM

PERIOD: Modern acquisition history
TYPE: historical_background
TAGS: archaeology, Mut Temple excavations, Amenhotep III statues

TEXT:
Numerous statues of Sekhmet have been discovered at the Temple of Mut and at sites associated with Amenhotep III in Luxor. Archaeological missions from organizations such as the American Research Center in Egypt and German expeditions have uncovered many of these sculptures during excavations in the Karnak region.



ID: gem_seated_sekhmet_symbolism_en_007
TITLE: Seated Statue of Goddess Sekhmet – Symbolism or Meaning
LANGUAGE: en
MUSEUM: GEM
EXHIBIT: Seated Statue of Goddess Sekhmet
PERIOD: New Kingdom – 18th Dynasty
TYPE: significance
TAGS: lioness goddess, Eye of Ra, healing goddess, divine protection

TEXT:
Sekhmet’s lioness head symbolizes fierce power and divine wrath. She was known as "The Powerful One" and served as the destructive aspect of the sun god Ra. At the same time, she was also associated with healing and protection. The ankh symbol represents life, while the solar disk crown—originally placed on her head—linked her directly to the sun god Ra.



ID: gem_seated_sekhmet_faq_en_008
TITLE: Seated Statue of Goddess Sekhmet – Common Questions
LANGUAGE: en
MUSEUM: GEM
EXHIBIT: Seated Statue of Goddess Sekhmet
PERIOD: New Kingdom – 18th Dynasty
TYPE: faq
TAGS: visitor questions, Sekhmet, Amenhotep III statues

TEXT:
Q: Why were there so many statues of Sekhmet?
A: Amenhotep III commissioned hundreds of statues—possibly one for each day of the year—to ensure the goddess was ritually appeased every day.

Q: What does the lion head represent?
A: It symbolizes Sekhmet’s fierce and destructive power as the "Eye of Ra."

Q: What does the ankh in her hand mean?
A: The ankh represents life and divine vitality.

Q: Where were these statues originally placed?
A: Many stood in the Temple of Mut at Karnak and other temples associated with Amenhotep III.

Q: Why is the solar disk missing?
A: The solar disk crown was originally inserted into a mortise on the head but is missing on many surviving statues.
