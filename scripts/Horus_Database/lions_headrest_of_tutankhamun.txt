TITLE: Lions Headrest – Overview
LANGUAGE: en
MUSEUM: GEM
EXHIBIT: Lions Headrest
PERIOD: New Kingdom, Dynasty 18 (Tutankhamun)
TYPE: overview
TAGS: Tutankhamun, headrest, lions, Shu, funerary furniture

TEXT:
This ivory headrest from Tutankhamun’s burial features a slim base and a central pillar carved as the god Shu, who raises the curved head support. Two crouching lions flank Shu, symbolizing guardianship of the horizon.

LANGUAGE: en
MUSEUM: GEM
EXHIBIT: Lions Headrest
PERIOD: New Kingdom, Dynasty 18 (Tutankhamun)
TYPE: details
TAGS: ivory carving, Shu, horizon guardians, Egyptian symbolism

TEXT:
Shu, god of air, is shown kneeling and lifting the headrest with raised arms, while the lions represent the eastern and western horizons. Black-painted details on Shu’s hair and kilt enhance the visual contrast and symbolic depth.



ID: gem_tut_lions_headrest_function_en_003
TITLE: Lions Headrest – Religious Meaning
LANGUAGE: en
MUSEUM: GEM
EXHIBIT: Lions Headrest
PERIOD: New Kingdom, Dynasty 18 (Tutankhamun)
TYPE: significance
TAGS: rebirth, cosmic order, afterlife belief

TEXT:
Headrests were linked to rebirth and awakening, as lifting the head symbolized rising to new life. The lions and Shu together express cosmic balance and protection during the king’s transition to the afterlife.
