TITLE: Canopic Coffinette of Tutankhamun – Isis
LANGUAGE: en
MUSEUM: GEM
EXHIBIT: Canopic Coffinette (Isis)
PERIOD: New Kingdom, Dynasty 18 (Tutankhamun)
TYPE: overview
TAGS: Tutankhamun, canopic coffinette, Isis, funerary gold

TEXT:
This miniature gold coffin is one of four canopic coffinettes used to hold Tutankhamun’s embalmed internal organs inside the canopic chest. Made of solid beaten gold and decorated with red carnelian and colored glass inlays, it shows the king wearing the nemes headcloth and holding the crook and flail.

TEXT:
A vertical column of hieroglyphic text runs down the front of the coffinette and contains a protective spell spoken by the goddess Isis. Isis was revered as a divine protector and healer, making her presence essential in rituals of rebirth and resurrection.

TYPE: significance
TAGS: embalmed organs, Four Sons of Horus, royal burial

TEXT:
Canopic coffinettes preserved the king’s internal organs under the guardianship of the Four Sons of Horus and their associated goddesses. This coffinette’s connection to Isis emphasizes her role in safeguarding the king’s physical essence for eternal life.

EXHIBIT: Canopic Coffinette (Isis)
PERIOD: New Kingdom, Dynasty 18 (Tutankhamun)
TYPE: historical_background
TAGS: repurposed object, altered cartouches, royal workshops

TEXT:
Evidence of altered cartouches indicates that this coffinette was repurposed, reflecting how royal workshops reused existing objects to complete Tutankhamun’s burial quickly. Despite this adaptation, its goldwork and protective inscription retained full religious effectiveness.
